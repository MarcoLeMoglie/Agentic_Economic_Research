\begin{frame}{Anatomy of a project}

      {\footnotesize INDEX: map and restart point.\\
      CURRENT\_STATE: where it stands.\\
      NEXT\_ACTIONS: what comes next.\\
      AGENT\_MEMORY: what the next agent must know.\par}
      \vspace{0.12em}
      \group{Read when needed}
      {\footnotesize PROJECT\_BRIEF: question and scope.\\
      DECISIONS: date, context, decision, reason.\\
      TECHNICAL\_NOTES: commands, bugs, fixes.\\
      SOURCES: where facts come from.\par}
      \vspace{0.12em}
      \group{History}
      {\footnotesize LOG, by date. SESSION\_SUMMARIES, one paragraph. PROMPTS worth reusing.\par}

\begin{frame}{Keeping your brain healthy}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \column{0.62\textwidth}
      {\small
      1. Save what the next session needs, not every conversation.\\[0.28em]
      2. Update state and decisions at every close.\\[0.28em]
      3. Audit on a schedule, and read the audit.\\[0.28em]
      4. Keep the source next to each claim.\\[0.28em]
      5. Commit before an agent edits; read the diff after.\\[0.28em]
      6. Keep private material out of anything you share or sync.\\[0.28em]
      7. Write repeated procedures as skills.\\[0.28em]
      8. Let an agent do the maintenance, with a snapshot first.\par}
    \column{0.34\textwidth}
      {\small\color{muted} JARVIS remembered because the script said so. Your assistant remembers what is in the files.\par}
  \end{columns}
  \takeaway{How useful an assistant is depends on the model, and just as much on the memory, tools and procedures around it.}
\end{frame}
