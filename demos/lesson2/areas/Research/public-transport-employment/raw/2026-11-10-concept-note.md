# Rivervale BRT and Employment — Concept Note

**SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT.** Rivervale, its statistical office, its transport authority, and all figures below are invented.

## Background

Rivervale is a fictional metropolitan area of 24 districts. Since 2017 the regional government has been opening a bus rapid transit network in phases. Stations are not distributed evenly: some districts received several stations early, others one station late or none. The stated policy goal is to reduce car dependence and improve access to jobs.

## Policy question

Does a substantial improvement in public transport access raise the formal employment rate of district residents? The government's assumption is yes. The counterargument is that stations follow growth rather than cause it, and that new housing near stations changes who lives there.

## Plausible mechanisms

1. **Access to jobs.** Faster trips widen the set of jobs a resident can reach within an acceptable commute.
2. **Cost of work.** A cheaper, more reliable commute raises the net return to taking a job, especially for second earners.
3. **Residential sorting.** New residents may move in for the transport amenity; the district's employment rate may change without anyone changing jobs.
4. **Firm location.** Employers may cluster near stations, raising local employment while displacing it elsewhere in the metro area.

Mechanisms 3 and 4 mean a district-level effect is not the same as a metro-wide effect.

## What a credible study would need

- Station opening dates, separated from announcement and construction start.
- Employment and population measured consistently at district level over a long pre-period.
- A comparison group of districts not yet served, with a defensible parallel-trends argument.
- Explicit rules for partial rollouts.
